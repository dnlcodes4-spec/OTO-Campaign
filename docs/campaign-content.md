## 02–04 — When You Get There, What Do You Have in Mind for Us?

This is one question most people going to the senate from Oyo South never answered before they took their seat. Little wonder their four or more years were usually uneventful and their names are never appended to any piece of legislation. So, my legislative agenda:

### 1. State Police Legislation

To work with colleagues to bring out legislation authorizing the creation of State Police, achieved via one of two possible routes:

**(a) Cross-caucus negotiation** — Discuss variously and differently with senators across the six geopolitical zones, jointly with both the Northern and Southern caucuses, to secure buy-in ahead of introducing the legislation. Seek their understanding and disabuse the notion that state police would aid secession. Assure them that state police will start with non-lethal force of arms (e.g. pistols with a 12-round max) and use of APCs (armored personnel carriers) for maximum protection of personnel and cap the number of recruits per population sample.

**(b) Redraft the Civil Defence law** — Work jointly with colleagues to redraft the law setting up the Civil Defence corps so it becomes a **regional force rather than a national force**:
- All personnel return to their regions and change their nomenclature.
- Operational law empowers them to prosecute certain crimes directly, independent of the police.
- The current Civil Defence structure becomes **six independent regional commands**.
- The six regions take turns nominating a Commandant General of the entire force on a **rotation basis between North and South** (not by ballot, to avoid corruption/bribery). Rotation starts with the region opposite of the sitting president's zone, then flips when that president's term ends.
- **Funding:** 30% federal, 70% states — split equally among the states within each zone.
- Operationally answerable to each state governor, giving each governor a standing force to call on for threats to peace in that state.
- Notes that a state police bill is already in the works nationally; OTO is ready to work with like minds to amend/improve it.

### 2. Constitutional Amendment — Residency Over State of Origin

Change the constitution to recognize **residency rather than state of origin**:
- Anyone who has lived an entire lifetime in a state should be able to claim legal residency there and receive the benefits that accrue to indigenes.
- Their children become naturalized indigenes of that state by virtue of birth.
- Rationale: eases ethnic tension, engenders patriotism, and encourages economic contribution to the state one actually lives in (framed around Oyo State, but applies nationally).
- Example: if Hausas, Fulanis, Igbos, Efiks, Ibibios, Angas, Ebiras, etc. living in Oyo State paid all their taxes to Oyo State and participated in its treasury, the state would be markedly better off.
- Also fixes **census/population accuracy**: currently people travel back to their state of origin to be counted rather than being counted where they actually live and draw resources, which distorts allocations and infrastructure planning (e.g. road projects) per state.

### 3. Constitutional Amendment — Secularity of the Nigerian State

Change the constitution to **expressly provide for the secularity of the Nigerian state** and remove religion from all government policy:
- Categorically state that no one may govern Nigeria through the tenets of any particular religion, with prescribed punishment (including impeachment) for violations.
- Comparative note: neighboring countries avoid Nigeria's religious tensions despite significant Muslim populations — Benin Republic (~60% Muslim), Sierra Leone (~61% Muslim), Burkina Faso (~60% Muslim) — because their constitutions explicitly declare secular statehood, keeping religion a private/home matter.
- Effect: anyone could become president or prime minister regardless of religion, since religion would carry less weight in governance; governance would be "by constitution and not by religious laws."

### 4. Restructuring — Six Geopolitical Zones as Federating Units

To make Nigeria "truly a republic and truly federating in spirit and letter":
- Cannot return to the 1963 regional lines, but can start with the **six geopolitical zones as federating units** of the republic, each free to choose its own name (e.g. Oduduwa Republic, Biafra Republic, etc.) — intended to neutralize unending secessionist agitation.
- Each zone controls its own education and economy.
- The center (federal level) jointly retains a single foreign ministry, Army, immigration, and Central Bank.
- A single currency with **regional variation** — e.g. a Biafran Naira equals an Oduduwa Naira equals all other Naira, all bearing the same symbol, just regionally denominated.

## 05 — Constituency Benefits & Development Pledges

Benefits of having a decent, honest senator representing Oyo South:
- Scholarships to deserving students.
- Renovation of schools/classrooms and provision of teaching aids.
- Assistance to farmers: fighting for adequate fertilizer supply to the Southern district, and syndicating loans for tractor purchases after reorganizing farmers into sectional associations.
- Emphasis that **not everything requires money** — much of society's problems stem from a lack of planning.

**Traffic/transport plan for the district:**
- Reroute buses so they don't all start and terminate at the same two spots (e.g. some continuing to start at Dugbe and end at Mokola but branching into inner roads for better accessibility).
- Help find investors for rail carriages to run within the city once existing tracks are ready — NRC has already liberalized carriage provision. Proposed short-travel rail line: Omi-Adio, Apata, Dugbe, Mokola, Bodija, etc., to ease road congestion.
- Attract investors for multi-level car parking facilities across Ibadan to further ease congestion.
- Provide additional transformers to improve electricity supply across communities in Oyo South.

Framed as low/no personal cost to the candidate — mainly about connecting existing opportunities with investors.

## Who Are We Looking For (Voter Engagement)

- Oyo State has about **4 million registered voters**, but only about a third are consistently active/participating.
- Roughly **3 million** do not participate. Reasons identified:
  1. Little confidence in the electoral process.
  2. Feeling that elections are reduced to vote-buying/bidding, and not wanting to "soil their hands" or take part in the charade.
  3. Feeling it's a waste of time.

### Addressing those concerns
- Under the new **BVAS** (INEC's Bimodal Voter Accreditation System), rigging can no longer be as brazen as before — no more thumbprinting while camped in a hotel, for instance — and (unlike in the 2023 elections) the campaign says it will have adequate hands to assist with results at polling units.
- Even attempts to rig at collation centers should be checkable, since original polling-unit vote counts must tally with what's collated.
- Even if wrong results are announced, overturning them is "a lot easier at the tribunal."
- If citizens do their part at the polls, tribunal challenges shouldn't even be necessary.
